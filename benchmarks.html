How to use this table

A practical reference for contractor conversations. Workers’ compensation values below are California WCIRB advisory pure premium rates effective September 1, 2026. General liability (GL) values are published premium-to-revenue examples converted into dollars per $1,000 of revenue. They are not carrier quotes and are not all California-specific.

Calculation: GL cost per $1,000 revenue = annual GL premium ÷ annual gross revenue × 1,000. So 1.0% of revenue equals $10 per $1,000. WC cost per $100 payroll is the classification's advisory pure premium rate before carrier pricing and account-specific factors.
Important: Do not use these rows as a pass/fail test or a promise of savings. WCIRB advisory pure premium rates are not the rates charged by a carrier. Carrier WC pricing, experience modification, schedule credits/debits, minimum premiums, fees and payroll allocation affect the final premium. GL benchmarks vary with classification, revenue, subcontracting, claims, operations, limits, deductible, endorsements and state.

3. How the diagnostic should use the references

  1. Calculate the contractor’s actual WC premium per $100 of payroll and GL premium per $1,000 of revenue using the intake data.
  2. Compare WC against the appropriate California class code(s), not the statewide average or an unrelated trade.
  3. Compare GL only against a trade- and exposure-appropriate reference. Flag national data as directional, not California-specific.
  4. Show the difference as a “review indicator,” not a savings estimate or proof of overpayment.
  5. Confirm all classification assignments, carrier rates, modifiers, minimum premiums, fees, policy limits and forms before recommending a remarket.
